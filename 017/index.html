Fourteen performance PRs to dev, and a plan to merge them

Since late September, the profiling behind this series has produced fourteen pull requests to dev: statics, loading text, the evaluator, the worker, the editor, and CI. Each is small, measured and tested, and none has been reviewed. This report is for whoever reviews them (Cyrus, Alexander, Andrew, Matt K): for each PR, what it buys and where that was measured, the honest reasons not to merge it yet, and what to look at; then the order to merge them in, which ones depend on or conflict with which, and what each needs first.

Short answers. The PRs as of 2026-10-15 evening, against dev at e12145c69b. Every number below was measured in its PR (or in the report named), each against dev or the livelits branch as it says.

  1. All fourteen are green and merge cleanly onto today's dev, one at a time. Every one passes CI (five checks pass and one is skipped, on each; dev requires none to merge, ^report(6)) and GitHub calls each mergeable. None has a review or a comment from a person. A fifteenth, #2608, is open too, and was missing from the list this report began from (below).
  2. What they buy, on dev: statics of nested functions from exponential to linear (5.3 s to 13 ms at eight levels; real programs 20 to 30% faster, #2603); loading text 2.5× faster and the parse test groups 267 s to 142 s (#2614, with #2610 for the fallback, 182 s to 63 s); re-evaluation after an edit 2.7 s to 0.41 s in a 400-definition program (#2673); and typed code that no longer copies or re-walks its values (#2661, #2679, #2681). On the livelits branches the same changes are larger: a Polygons click 15 s to 2 s, a Kids' Choice release 1.6 s to 0.8 s (Figure 1).
  3. The reasons to wait are few and specific. Three pairs conflict textually (#2610 with #2614, #2661 with #2679, #2673 with #2681), and three PRs conflict with Andrew's #2469 (#2603, #2606, #2614). #2681 contains #2661 and #2679. #2603 and #2673 are the two to review most carefully: the largest statics change, and a new general hashing mechanism. #2490 is a spike, not a merge candidate (Figure 2).
  4. The merge plan: four waves, small and independent first, the evaluator together, and the two that need coordination last. Merged in that order, 9 of the fourteen go in with no conflict; 5 need a hand resolution, two of them only the test list. With those resolutions all fifteen together build and pass the full test suite, 4,756 tests (the plan).
