Hazel performance analysis

Notes on making Hazel responsive enough for its livelits to feel like plain apps: what we measured, what we found, what we fixed. Matthew Hammer.

  1. Issue 003 · 2026-10-11
    A day of requests from M and N

    My daughter and son asked for features all afternoon, and each went live in the game as soon as it was ready: claws and star points to 100, longer arms, backgrounds, Daniel Tiger's stripes, a witch, a wolf and a deer. The hands and the mouth have already reached the Hazel slides, where one alien hand with 99 fingers takes 32 s to redraw, against 5 ms in the game.

  2. Issue 002 · 2026-10-11
    From a game to a slide deck: Messed Up Dress Up in Hazel

    With slider releases down to about 0.13 s, the playground slides could carry more. Each feature was tried first in a one-file HTML/JS game, by its players, then formulated in Hazel: hair on the head, a neck, arms with a shirt that is a system of polygons, hands, eyes and a mouth, and two outline modes for building and for playing. What each cost, and why the heaviest slide now spends its time printing points.

  3. Issue 001 · 2026-10-10
    Why a slider isn't instant yet

    A livelit slider release took 4.6 s in the afternoon of 2026-10-09 and takes about 0.4 s now, against under a millisecond in a plain HTML/JS app. Every performance problem found this fall, its fix, where the remaining 0.4 s goes, and what it would take to get to "immediate".
